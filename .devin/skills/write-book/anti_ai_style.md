# Editorial Style and Truthful Voice

Applies to manuscript, captions, cover copy and metadata. This filename is kept
for compatibility; the goal is clear, credible writing, not detector evasion.
Style never overrides accuracy, source attribution, required disclosures or the
acceptance gates in `SKILL.md`.

## Explain before compressing

- Use concrete language, active voice and varied sentence/paragraph length.
  Short paragraphs are useful; a whole chapter of compressed assertions is not.
- Develop mechanisms, reasoning, assumptions and trade-offs. Put the reader
  through a complete example instead of repeating that a method works.
- Use tables for comparisons, lists for steps, and prose for explanation. Do not
  replace the explanation with headings, slogans, blank forms or bullet fragments.
- Define unfamiliar technical terms at first relevant use. Reuse terms consistently.
- Avoid generic hype, empty transitions and repetitive chapter formulas. Expressions
  such as "game-changer", "unlock" and "in today's fast-paced world" usually add
  no meaning. Evaluate words in context rather than applying a blind ban.
- Avoid excessive em dashes, constant "not X but Y" constructions and repeated
  machine/lie/theater metaphors. Do not force identical three-item lists.
- Keep formatting and terminology consistent. Never introduce deliberate errors
  or arbitrary inconsistency to simulate a human voice.

## Provenance is part of voice

- Specificity must be earned: real observations require sources; constructed
  numbers/scenarios must be clearly labeled illustrative, not passed off as results.
- Use first-person experience only when the author supplied or explicitly confirmed
  that specific account. Never infer a witnessed incident from a CV or job title.
- Do not call an invented event "anonymized". Anonymization protects a real account;
  a constructed scenario is an illustrative example. A composite needs provenance
  for its claimed factual components and must not imply a single witnessed event.
- Label examples locally, including their charts/tables. A preface disclaimer does
  not cure a chapter's false claim of personal observation or measured success.
- Distinguish advice, assumptions, opinion and verified facts. State uncertainty
  where it matters; never replace missing evidence with a confident tone.
- Keep irrelevant production chatter out of reader-facing prose, but do not claim
  exclusive human authorship or suppress required platform disclosures. Keep
  provenance records truthful and separate from the reader-facing manuscript.

## Final style check

Read for comprehension and usefulness, not just banned words. Ask whether the
reader can follow the reasoning, perform the promised task, and recognize the
method's limits. Stylistic polish cannot compensate for missing substance.
